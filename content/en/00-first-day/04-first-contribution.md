# 0.4 My first contribution

## Problem

You found something confusing in this course. How do you tell the team in a way that nobody forgets and everything stays organized, without it getting lost in a sea of chat messages?

## Blocks

### 1. What an issue is
An issue is a record of a problem, a question or an idea inside a repository. It sits in a list, people can comment on it, and it is closed once it is solved.

> **Tutor notes:** analogy: a sticky note that never falls off the wall and the whole team can see.

### 2. How to write a good issue
A good issue answers three questions:
- **What happened?**
- **What did you expect?**
- **Where was it?** (which lesson, which block)

> **Tutor notes:** show a bad example ("it doesn't work") and a good one. Ask the student to improve the bad example.

### 3. This is already real work
Opening clear issues is a real, valued skill. Software teams live on good reports. By opening your issue, you are already contributing to an Allerac project.

> **Tutor notes:** celebrate. This is the student's first public record in a real project.

## Mission

1. Open an issue in the [allerac-academy](https://github.com/Allerac/allerac-academy/issues) repository.
2. Say what you thought of module 0: what was clear, what was confusing and what you would change.
3. Use the format: what happened, what you expected and where it was.
4. You can write the title and text in your own language: issues about the course content are content, not code.

**How the tutor checks:** the issue exists, has a clear title and follows the format.
