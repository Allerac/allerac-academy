# 1.2 When AI makes things up

## Problem

You ask AI for a list of books on a topic, with author and year. It gives you 5 books, nicely formatted. You look one of them up online and it does not exist. What happened?

## Blocks

### 1. Hallucination
When AI confidently generates something false, we call it a hallucination. It is not lying: it is generating what seems likely, and sometimes what is likely is not real.

> **Tutor notes:** connect it to lesson 1.1: AI predicts, it does not look things up. Books, quotes, links and numbers are where it makes things up the most.

### 2. Where it gets things wrong most
- Very specific facts: names, dates, numbers, links.
- Recent things that happened after it learned.
- Code that uses tools it does not know well.

> **Tutor notes:** give a code example: AI may call a function that does not exist. This will come up a lot in practice.

### 3. How to check
- Ask for the source and open the link.
- Test it: we run code to see if it works.
- Ask in a different way and compare.
- When in doubt, be suspicious of very precise details.

> **Tutor notes:** stress that checking is part of the job, not a sign of distrust. The testing modules go deeper into this.

### 4. You are the one who signs off
AI is a fast coworker, but you are the one who delivers the work. Being good with AI is, to a large extent, being good at checking.

## Mission

1. Ask AI for 3 fun facts about your city, with sources.
2. Check at least one of them online.
3. Tell the tutor: was it right? Did the source exist?

**How the tutor checks:** the student checked at least one fact and reported the result, right or wrong.
