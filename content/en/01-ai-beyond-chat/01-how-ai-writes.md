# 1.1 How AI writes

## Problem

Finish the sentence: "It's raining today, so I'll take my..."

You probably thought of "umbrella". Why that word, and not "pineapple"?

## Blocks

### 1. Predicting the next word
A language model (like Claude or GPT) does exactly what you just did: it looks at the text so far and predicts the most likely next piece. Then it repeats, piece by piece, until the whole answer is written.

> **Tutor notes:** stress that the idea is simple, but the result is powerful because of scale. Do not get into neural networks. The "pieces" are called tokens; mention it only if the student asks.

### 2. It learned by reading a lot
The model learned these patterns by reading a huge amount of text: books, websites, and a lot of code. That is why it writes well in English, in many other languages and in programming languages.

> **Tutor notes:** analogy: someone who has read almost everything ever written but has never left the house. They know a lot about the world, but they cannot see what is happening right now.

### 3. Code is text too
To AI, code is just another kind of text, with very strict rules. It has seen millions of examples, so it can write code from a request in everyday language.

> **Tutor notes:** this is the core idea of the course: you describe what you want, and the AI writes the code. Your job is to ask well and to check.

### 4. It does not "know", it predicts
AI does not look things up in an encyclopedia or think like a person. It generates what seems most likely. Most of the time, the most likely answer is also the right one. But not always.

> **Tutor notes:** this block sets up the next lesson. Do not go deep.

## Mission

Ask AI: "Explain how you generate an answer, as if I were 10 years old." Then tell the tutor, in your own words, one thing you did not know.

**How the tutor checks:** the student explains, in their own words, that AI predicts the next piece of text.
